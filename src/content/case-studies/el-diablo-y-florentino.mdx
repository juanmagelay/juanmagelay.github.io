## Overview

El Diablo y Florentino is a **third-person soulslike survival horror shooter for PC, built in Unreal Engine**. It draws on the Venezuelan legend of Florentino and the Devil and on Canserbero's song “Es épico”.

For the Business Planning course in the Video Game Development and Simulation degree at UNAHUR, we had to **turn the initial game concept into a plan an investor could evaluate**.

**The scenario is hypothetical.** The course asked us to pitch the business as if the three of us were an indie studio raising money. In reality, the game is Jonathan Puyol's personal project, which he's building in Unreal. I'm not a developer on the game.

- **My role:** ideation, secondary research, player persona, competitor analysis, SWOT, MDA framework, Value Proposition Canvas, business and marketing plans with costs, Gantt and roadmap, plus the full deck design and visual system.
- **Team:** Jonathan Puyol, Damián Bruque and me.
- **Course:** Business Planning, Video Game Development and Simulation degree, UNAHUR, 2026. Professor: Nahir Acuña.
- **Timeline:** 2 months, across two midterms.
- **Tools:** FigJam, Claude Desktop, Claude Design, Google Slides, Google Sheets, Google Gemini.

## The challenge

Turn a game concept into **a business plan that holds up in front of an investor**, for a studio with no budget and no publisher.

**I could apply my Product Design mindset fully to an indie game**: define the user, prove the fit, size the investment and define likely scenarios.

The work was delivered in two midterms:

- **First midterm:** product, market and player.
- **Second midterm:** business model, marketing plan, costs and roadmap.

## Market and player research

The closest competitors, Resident Evil Veronica, Silent Hill Townfall and Gears of War: E-Day, all launch in 2026 or 2027. The SWOT put the studio's weak spots on the table: no budget, a small team, missing roles and no publisher. It also found the opening: **horror games spread fast on Twitch and YouTube**.

For the player, **I worked from secondary research**: the Quantic Foundry Gamer Motivation Model (over 1.25M players), inStreamly's Gamer Demographics 2026 and Reddit threads from soulslike communities.

<Figure
  src={segment}
  alt="Slide with seven figures in gold on dark cards: a median age of 23, 38% of the global market, 74% men in the Quantic Foundry sample, over 420 million players in Latin America, 80% adults who study or work, 69% core gamers and 908 million PC players worldwide."
  caption="The segment in numbers, from Quantic Foundry and inStreamly."
/>

That research became Diego Ramírez: 23, a university student in Buenos Aires who plays on PC through Steam. He plays 2 to 5 hour sessions most days, buys DLCs and learns from lore channels and speedrunners on YouTube. His Bartle profile is Explorer and Achiever: **he wants to soak in the world, then beat it**.

<Figure
  src={persona}
  alt="User persona slide for Diego Ramírez: a round portrait of a man with dark hair and stubble in front of a gaming PC, his Explorer and Achiever Bartle profile, a quote about mastering what is hard, demographic chips (23, man, Buenos Aires, university student, middle income, PC and Steam) and two market segments."
  caption="Diego Ramírez, built from Quantic Foundry, inStreamly and Reddit evidence."
/>

**His pains came with evidence**, most of them linked to a Reddit thread:

- Fights decided by luck instead of skill.
- Input lag.
- Delayed attacks and groups of hyper-aggressive enemies.
- Losing souls or runes to physics glitches or imprecise platforming.

The rule underneath: **the game can be brutal, but it has to be fair**. Every pain Diego described was about losing to the game, not to the challenge. The marketing plan turned that rule into a KPI: 85% positive reviews on Steam.

## Go to market

Development runs from January to August 2027, with a demo at Steam Next Fest in June. **Launch lands in late October 2027, timed to Halloween**, Steam Scream Fest and EVA, Argentina's game industry expo in Buenos Aires.

<Figure
  src={roadmap}
  alt="Marketing roadmap slide: a timeline from January 2027 to March 2028 with milestones such as the landing page in January, the Steam page in February, Steam Next Fest in June and the launch with EVA and Scream Fest in October, above four cost boxes for pre-launch, launch, post-launch and the community manager."
  caption="From Steam Next Fest in June to a late October launch."
/>

The campaign is called “No hay salida” (there's no way out) because **that's what the Devil wants you to believe in the game**, and it carries that promise through its three phases:

1. **“Firmá el pacto” (sign the pact):** a newsletter that unlocks early access to the demo.
2. **“El contrapunteo”:** a speedrun duel against the demo's boss. It takes its name from the legend, where Florentino and the Devil face off in a *contrapunteo*: a duel of improvised sung verses. The top 10 players appear in the game's credits.
3. **Launch trailer:** scored with heavy metal, Diego's music.

<Figure
  src={campaign}
  alt="Audience and campaign concept slide. On the left, the target audience with Diego Ramírez's portrait and chips for age, gender, platform and region. On the right, the concept “No hay salida” and three banners for its phases: Firmá el pacto, El contrapunteo and No hay salida."
  caption="“No hay salida”, the campaign concept, in its three phases."
/>

The first marketing plan cost USD 26,183. **I brought it down to the floor of 15% of development cost**, in line with what a mid-size indie studio is willing to spend on marketing. We cut a trip to Gamescom Latam in São Paulo, TikTok ads and paid previews with YouTubers.

**Channels follow the persona.** Paid media goes to YouTube and Reddit, where Diego watches gameplay videos and joins forum discussions. Fan support goes through Cafecito and Ko-fi, but we left it out of the budget: it would be an extra.

## Visual system

I built **a dark fantasy HUD aesthetic from the team's own reference pieces**.

I started from the game's hell: **a dark, warm base that lets the character's red eyes glow**. Text is set in parchment, with high contrast on soot. The metals set the hierarchy: old gold is kept for the numbers an investor looks at. Blood red stays under 5%, so it always means danger or health, like in a HUD.

## Deck storytelling

**The deck runs in four acts**: the game, the market, the player and the business, with the marketing plan inside Act IV. A health bar shows progress.

The first midterm ended on a teaser. The character stares out, eyes glowing red, over one question: “Are you investing or not?” The second midterm opens Act IV with the same question and **closes by answering it with the numbers**. The final slide lands on “Firmá el pacto”, the campaign's call to action.

## How I worked with AI

I set up a Claude Desktop project as the context base: general instructions, previous decks, game captures, the ideation FigJam and the secondary research. **Every draft started from that context instead of a blank prompt.**

With Claude Desktop and Claude Design I generated first drafts of the decks and the budget spreadsheet, including slides built with code. **Then I finished everything by hand**: I changed the design, edited the spreadsheets and the numbers, and verified every figure.

I defined the system, the context and the criteria. AI sped up production. **Final touch-ups by hand were still necessary.**

## What I learned

- **Numbers are part of the design.** A figure that doesn't match its source weakens every slide around it.
- **Show the number that hurts.** The break-even slide made the plan more credible.
- **A system lets several authors ship one artifact.** Without it, the second midterm would have been three decks stapled together.
- **Back every decision about the user, the market and the business with its original source** (even a secondary one). It kept building our credibility throughout the course.
